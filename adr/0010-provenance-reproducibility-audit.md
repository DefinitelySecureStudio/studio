# ADR 0010: Provenance, reproducibility, and audit requirements

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-16
- Decision owners: Definitely Secure Studio
- Related issue: [#43 — Define provenance, reproducibility, and audit requirements](https://github.com/DefinitelySecureStudio/studio/issues/43)
- Parent epic: [#3 — Studio Constitution](https://github.com/DefinitelySecureStudio/studio/issues/3)
- Constitutional article: [Provenance, reproducibility, and audit](../CONSTITUTION.md#7-provenance-reproducibility-and-audit)

## Context

AI-assisted production combines authoritative creative sources, private context,
instructions, hosted models, tools, human selection and editing, deterministic
build steps, and exact released files. A digest of the final output does not
explain how it was made, while a saved prompt does not prove that a stochastic
service can produce the same result again.

The Studio needs evidence strong enough to audit creative and technical work
without making false reproducibility claims or copying secrets and Lore into
public manifests. The Constitution must define outcomes and minimum evidence;
Codex and repository policies will define schemas and implementation details.

## Decision

Distinguish three obligations:

1. **Traceability** follows an artifact backward to its inputs, mechanisms,
   decisions, and approvals and follows an input or decision forward to affected
   outputs.
2. **Exact reproducibility** recreates byte-identical output from pinned inputs
   and a recorded controlled procedure.
3. **Auditability** lets an authorized reviewer verify what happened, why, under
   whose authority, with what controls and evidence, and with what result even
   when exact regeneration is impossible.

Every material artifact is traceable and auditable. Deterministic release
assembly, packaging, manifests, and derived transformations are exactly
reproducible. Nondeterministic generations need not reproduce exactly, but the
exact selected candidate is preserved and all deterministic downstream changes
are reproducible. Every workflow declares an exact, partial, or audit-only
classification and its boundary.

Use one common provenance model identifying artifact identity and sensitivity,
immutable input references, governing policy/specification/prompt versions,
human and mechanism identities, material parameters and environment, ordered
transformations, validation and failure evidence, decisions and approvals, and
exact output identity. Unknown, unavailable, inapplicable, and intentionally
withheld values remain distinguishable.

Add class-specific evidence for generated text/code, generated visual and
time-based media, authored creative sources, manifests and reports, Lore context
packages, software/build artifacts, and published releases. Deterministic
metadata and context payloads must reproduce exactly, while randomized signature,
timestamp, encryption, or transport envelopes may vary if payload identity is
verifiable.

For nondeterministic generation, record all exposed identifiers and controls,
preserve the exact returned candidate before edits, and represent selection and
human edits as separate lineage events. Provider opacity and unavailable fields
are explicit limitations. A seed is evidence rather than proof. Each rerun is a
new event and cannot overwrite or claim to reproduce the original.

Before publication, create an immutable release record covering release and
output identity, provenance contract, exact inputs and source revisions,
workflow/build identity, model/tool versions, validations, approvals, canon
scope, rights notices, reproducibility classification and instructions, and
known gaps. Public records contain only an opaque, non-derivable attestation for
private influence; authorized reviewers follow it to a paired restricted record.

Audit records are append-only or equivalently tamper-evident, attributable,
time-ordered, integrity-verifiable, and sensitivity-appropriate. Corrections
append rather than overwrite. Provenance completeness and integrity are checked
before canon promotion and publication, and the producing agent's self-report
is not the sole verifier of consequential actions.

Exclude secret values, credentials, private keys, unnecessary personal data,
and protected content from provenance records. Store private evidence only in a
paired restricted record when needed, minimize retention, and keep public links
opaque. Each record class has an owner, purpose, access policy, retention event,
preservation duties, and deletion method. Official release identity and lineage
remain preserved even after withdrawal or deprecation.

## Rationale

The three-part model avoids equating a saved prompt with reproducibility and
avoids rejecting valuable nondeterministic work merely because it cannot be
regenerated byte-for-byte. Preserving the exact selected output plus its lineage
supports meaningful review, correction, and defense.

Common fields make audit expectations consistent across media while the class
matrix captures material differences. Paired public and restricted records
preserve verifiability without turning provenance into a disclosure channel.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Releases identify the exact inputs, specifications, mechanisms, approvals,
  and bytes that produced them.
- Reproducibility claims have a precise, testable meaning and stated boundary.
- Nondeterministic work remains auditable without pretending a rerun is the same
  artifact.
- Lineage can identify every output affected by a source, tool, approval, or
  defect.
- Sensitive evidence remains separated from reader-safe public records.

### Tradeoffs

- Workflows must preserve selected candidates and material intermediate states.
- Release preparation includes provenance verification and retention review.
- Hosted-provider opacity may force an audit-only classification or block a
  release when the remaining evidence is insufficient.
- Restricted audit systems require durable access control and lifecycle
  management.

## Rejected alternatives

### Require every generated artifact to reproduce exactly

Rejected because hosted models and other stochastic systems may not expose or
control every influence, even when their selected output is fully preserved and
auditable.

### Treat a prompt and seed as sufficient provenance

Rejected because models, provider infrastructure, tools, source context,
selection, editing, and safety transformations also affect the result.

### Store complete prompts and inputs in public manifests

Rejected because self-contained public records would leak private Lore,
personal data, secrets, and sensitive security evidence.

### Keep only the final released files

Rejected because exact bytes prove identity but not authority, source rights,
creative lineage, approvals, or the reason a result was selected.

### Use mutable logs as the audit authority

Rejected because overwritten or unauthenticated logs cannot establish a durable
sequence of actions and decisions.

## Implementation

The Constitution owns the normative evidence, reproducibility, audit, and
retention requirements. The existing release-provenance policy implements the
public/restricted record split. Codex will own machine-readable contracts;
Universe, Lore, and Platform will own records in their respective domains.
